# Pattern Templates — Streetwise Translations

Five viral hook patterns extracted from the socialgrowthengineers.com dataset, translated into Streetwise operator voice. Each pattern includes the source structure, why it works, the Streetwise translation rule, and worked examples using our actual case studies from CLAUDE.md §7.

---

## Pattern 1 — Specific number → specific outcome

### Why it works
The viral dataset is full of micro-claims like "$196 spent · 5.0★ · 11 days". Performance hooks because the numbers are too specific to be fake. CLAUDE.md §6 already mandates "numbers over adjectives" — this pattern is the most natively-Streetwise of the five.

### Structure
`[Spend or input metric]. [Outcome metric]. [One-line mechanism naming the channel/step].`

Three beats. No connective tissue. Each beat is a fact.

### Streetwise voice rules
- Currency symbol attached to the number, never spelled out
- One mechanism named, not three (avoid "Meta + Google + LinkedIn = €72K" — pick the dominant channel)
- Time element optional but powerful when short (11 days, one Tuesday, 48 hours)

### Worked examples from §7 cases

**Dublin Beer Festival (Antonio's lead):**
> €4,639 in. €72,000 out. The Meta retargeting set ran for 11 days.

> 3,971 tickets at €1.22 each. Meta did 73% of it. The 27% Google handled was higher CPA but it filled the seats.

**Jobbio Career Fair (Antonio's lead):**
> €30,000 spend. €135,000 revenue. 60 MQLs. LinkedIn lead form beat the landing page 64% to 36%.

**SaaS A/B test (Antonio's lead):**
> CPL £149 → £34.70. We didn't change the audience. We changed the form.

**DP Gates (Antonio, Caruso Martech client):**
> $1,995 sale from a Meta native lead-gen ad. The form had four fields. Two of them were dropdowns.

### When to use
- Case-study posts on LinkedIn
- Founder posts on results (Antonio)
- Sales-led credibility builds (Sean adapting to his audience)
- Ad headlines for performance services
- First slide of a proposal opening case-study section

### When to avoid
- POV-led content (use Pattern 4 instead)
- Strategic/positioning posts where the takeaway is conceptual
- When no proof asset exists — invent nothing

## Pattern 2 — Provocative reframe

### Why it works
Top business hooks challenge a default assumption. The reader pauses because the line contradicts what they expected. In TikTok the bait is shock; in LinkedIn it's authority — the reader thinks "wait, is that true?" and reads on to test it.

### Structure
`[Common belief]. [Sharp contradiction or inversion].`

Two beats. The first sets up the assumption. The second flips it.

### Streetwise voice rules
- Never invert a belief Streetwise doesn't actually disagree with — credibility is the whole asset
- The flip must lead to a takeaway you can defend with evidence in the body
- Avoid contrarian-for-its-own-sake (that's the LinkedIn-bro failure mode); the flip should be operationally useful

### Worked examples

**Sean LinkedIn / outbound thought leadership:**
> Your LinkedIn outreach isn't broken. Your offer is.

> Sales Navigator filters won't fix a bad ICP. They just help you reach the wrong people faster.

**Antonio paid-media takes:**
> Stop A/B testing creatives. Test audiences first. Until the audience is right the creative doesn't matter.

> Performance Max isn't a black box. It's a leak detector — and most accounts have leaks.

**Strategy / positioning (joint):**
> Most agencies sell channels. Channels are commodity. We sell systems that make channels compound.

### When to use
- Sean's LinkedIn thought-leadership (his strongest pattern)
- Sales Navigator opener for cold prospects who've heard every pitch
- Battlecard one-liners against competitors
- Conference talk titles, podcast episode headlines

### When to avoid
- Any post about a specific client (use Pattern 1 or 3)
- Anywhere the contrarian framing isn't defensible in the body
- Direct-response ads (the flip is too cerebral for a cold audience)

## Pattern 3 — Inside-the-room

### Why it works
Show the actual mechanism. The viral version is unboxing or process content; ours is exposing the build. CLAUDE.md §6: "show the work — name the channel, the budget, the funnel step." This pattern is that rule made literal.

### Structure
`[Specific named asset/mechanism shown or described] + [the one thing that makes it work].`

The reader gets to see something they wouldn't normally see. The hook is the artifact itself, not a description of the artifact.

### Streetwise voice rules
- Name the actual tool, node, account, line of code, ad set — vague references kill it
- Reveal one mechanism per hook, not three (depth beats breadth)
- Pair with a screenshot or paste of the artifact whenever the channel allows

### Worked examples

**Antonio AI Workflows:**
> This is the exact n8n filter that took Sean's daily Upwork alerts from 80 to 12. Seven lines of code.

> The Claude scoring prompt I'm using for Upwork. It's three paragraphs. The category list does 80% of the work.

**Antonio paid media:**
> This is the LinkedIn Lead Gen form that took CPL from £149 to £34.70. Three field changes. The biggest one was making "company size" optional.

> Here's the Meta audience structure for the Dublin Beer Festival. Three retargeting tiers, no lookalike. The fourth tier killed performance — I cut it on day three.

**Antonio tracking:**
> The GA4 events Daniel needs to track for the Edelweisshütte campaign. Eight events. Five of them are conversion-worthy; three are diagnostic only.

**Sean LinkedIn outbound:**
> This is the opener that booked 40 calls in October. Two lines. The second line is a specific question, not a generic ask.

### When to use
- Antonio's founder LinkedIn posts (his strongest pattern)
- Process / how-we-do-it posts
- Carousel slide 1
- YouTube video thumbnails for Sean's channel
- Recap posts after client wins

### When to avoid
- Top-of-funnel cold content where the reader has no context for the artifact
- Posts under NDA or with client-identifying detail (anonymise first)

## Pattern 4 — Confession with a turn

### Why it works
Viral hooks open with self-deprecation or admission, then deliver a lesson. The pattern aligns with CLAUDE.md §6's "acknowledge trade-offs" rule. Readers trust people who name their mistakes — agencies almost never do. The contrast is the asset.

### Structure
`[Specific past mistake or assumption]. [What changed + what we do now].`

The first beat is concrete enough to be embarrassing. The second beat is a system-level lesson, not a platitude.

### Streetwise voice rules
- The mistake must be real. Don't manufacture humility — it reads false
- The "turn" must be operational. A specific framework, threshold, or process change. Not "I learned to listen more"
- Works best for founder positioning, not client-facing content

### Worked examples

**Antonio founder positioning:**
> I priced my first paid-media retainer at $750/month. I lost money on every client. The current floor is AUD $2,000 — and that's still a starter tier, not the rate.

> I bid the Jobbio Career Fair assuming it would be a paid social play. The LinkedIn lead form beat the landing page 64% to 36%. I was wrong about everything except the budget.

> For two years I built reports clients didn't read. Now I open every QBR with one chart and one decision. Engagement on the rest tripled.

**Sean founder positioning:**
> I used to send 300 connection requests a week with the same opener. Reply rate was 3%. The week I rewrote the opener around the prospect's specific funding round, replies hit 19%.

> I closed my first $30K deal with a 6-slide deck. The deck after that was 28 slides. The deck after THAT closed nothing.

**Strategy/branding:**
> Streetwise existed for six months before we had a real ICP. We took every retainer at $1,500 and lost money on half. The clearest thing we did in year one was double the floor and turn down four clients in a week.

### When to use
- Founder LinkedIn posts (both Antonio and Sean)
- Year-end / quarter-end reflection posts
- Podcast or conference talk openers
- Long-form Substack/blog intros

### When to avoid
- Direct response or sales content (confession lowers urgency)
- Client-facing case studies (we acknowledge OUR mistakes, not client missteps)
- Any context where the audience needs reassurance, not honesty

## Pattern 5 — Pattern interrupt

### Why it works
Top-outlier hooks lead with something that breaks the reader's scrolling cadence — a fragment, a number stack, a single word. On TikTok it's a sound cue. On LinkedIn it's a single line followed by aggressive whitespace. The hook *is* the disruption.

### Structure
`[Fragment, single word, or number stack]` on its own line. Then a hard break. Then the post.

The first line cannot be a sentence. It's typographically distinct from anything else on the feed.

### Streetwise voice rules
- Use sparingly. If every post opens this way the pattern stops interrupting
- The fragment must hint at a specific, payable result (not vague mystery)
- Works on LinkedIn and video thumbnails. Doesn't work in email subject lines (filters flag it)

### Worked examples

**Antonio paid-media reveals:**
> €72,000.
>
> From €4,639. Dublin. Beer. Eleven days.
>
> Here's the Meta audience structure that did it.

> 3,971.
>
> Tickets sold. €1.22 each. Two channels.

> CPL £149.
>
> Then we changed the form.
>
> CPL £34.70.

**Sean LinkedIn:**
> 40 calls.
>
> One opener. Two lines. Three weeks.

**Streetwise milestones:**
> 500.
>
> Qualified leads delivered. 95% retention. From a two-person agency.

### When to use
- Anniversary / milestone posts (year mark, deal closes, threshold crossings)
- Case-study reveals — Pattern 5 opens, Pattern 1 or 3 builds the body
- Video thumbnails (the fragment becomes the on-screen text)
- LinkedIn carousels (slide 1 = fragment, slide 2 = explanation)

### When to avoid
- Outbound DMs (looks gimmicky in a personal message)
- Email subject lines
- Proposal headers (too informal)
- More than once every 5-7 posts on the same channel

## Combination patterns (advanced)

Patterns combine. The most common pairings:

- **5 → 1**: Pattern interrupt as the hook, specific numbers as the body open. Best for case-study reveals.
- **2 → 3**: Provocative reframe as the hook, inside-the-room as the proof. Best for thought-leadership backed by mechanism.
- **4 → 3**: Confession as the hook, inside-the-room as the lesson. Best for founder posts that teach.

Do not combine more than two patterns in one piece. Three is mush.

## Generation checklist

Before returning any hook, run through these in order:

1. Did I pick the pattern based on the service line (per SKILL.md table)?
2. Does the hook reference a specific number, named asset, or named mechanism?
3. If results-led: is the number from CLAUDE.md §7 (real) or invented (forbidden)?
4. Banned words check (§6): no game-changing / unlock / supercharge / 10x / synergy / leverage-verb / delve / moreover?
5. Exclamation marks: none?
6. Em-dash (—) in body prose: none (this file uses them for *headings only*, never inside marketing copy)?
7. Generic "we help businesses" energy: none?
8. Author voice matches the actual writer (Sean / Antonio / Streetwise)?
9. Length: under 30 words for the hook itself (long is not viral)?
10. If LinkedIn: typographical break after the hook so it stops scrolling?

If any of these fail: regenerate. Do not ship a hook that fails the checklist.
