## Voice Characteristics (observed from real proposals)

| Trait | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| **Operator-confident, not salesy** | "I diagnose the real need first, build genuine rapport, and position the solution in a way that makes the decision easy rather than pressured." |
| **Mirrors the prospect's framing** | If the role says "task-based marketer," Sean opens with "I am not a task-based marketer." Always speaks back the prospect's exact wording. |

## External vs Internal Voice (CRITICAL distinction)

Sean has two voices. Don't confuse them.

| Audience | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **External (prospects, clients, Upwork)** | Direct, professional, structured prose, no slang | "I have spent over a decade closing deals and consulting across sales, digital media, and professional services." |
| **Internal (Slack with Antonio)** | Casual, profane, fast | "Fuck, just seeing this now" / "I probably would've charged 3k but fuck let's go for 4.5k" / "Tis one is close, mate" |

Never use the internal voice externally. Never tighten the internal voice when writing internal Slack — it should stay loose.

## Channel-by-Channel Rules

### Upwork Applications (250–600 words depending on stakes)
Pair this with `sw-proposal-format-router` Step 2A.

**The Sean structure (from real winning proposals):**
1. **Position-mirror opener** — restate the role/situation in your own words. Not "I'm excited" — but "I am not a task-based marketer..." or "I have spent over a decade closing deals..."
2. **Career credibility line** — Facebook Dublin, 1000+ deals, $X spend managed, name brands (Snapchat, Workday, Facebook).
3. **Specific case study with hard numbers** — Chefs Office Academy, 192 leads at $2.74 each, 90% CPL reduction.
4. **Reframe the work in operator-language** — "I think in levers, not deliverables" / "I diagnose the real need first."
5. **Mention Antonio when stakes/complexity warrant it** — Malta partner, complementary skills, timezone coverage.
6. **Conditional close** — "If you are looking for someone who can close at a high level and add genuine value to the client relationship beyond the transaction, I would be a strong fit."

**Banned openers (confirmed from analysis):**
- "I'm excited to apply..."
- "I'd love to help you..."
- "Hope this finds you well..."
- "I noticed your job post..."

**Approved openers (verbatim from Sean's wins):**
- "I have spent over a decade closing deals and consulting across..."
- "I am not a task-based marketer and this role is a strong fit for how I naturally operate."
- "Hi [name], the set up phase involves..." (when answering a specific prospect question)
- "Happy to work through this blind." (for blind diagnostics)

### Blind Diagnostics / Audit-Style Asks (≤300 words, bullet form)
When the prospect asks for a structured analytical reply (e.g., "answer in 4 bullets"):
- Open: "Happy to work through this blind."
- Use the structure they asked for — don't add bullets if they didn't ask
- Always include a **kill rule** ("If neither variant hits a CVR above the current baseline after 200 sessions each, I kill both")
- Always tie metrics to outcomes ("Blended CAC at a subscription business hides which acquisition source is profitable and which is subsidised by retention revenue")
- Sign off: "Seán" (with the accent) — not "Sean O'Brien | Co-founder"

### LinkedIn Outbound (cold messages)
- ≤80 words
- Named relevance hook in line 1
- One ask, never two
- No links in first message
- Sign off: "— Seán"

### LinkedIn Personal Posts
- ≤200 words for standard; up to 1300 chars when the story warrants it
- Hook in line 1 — tension or specific number
- Body: a real situation with numbers, not a framework
- Cadence: 4–8 per month
- Mix: 40% case study results · 30% tactics · 20% industry observation · 10% behind-the-scenes

### Cold Email
- ≤80 words body
- Subject: a specific question about THEIR business, not "quick question"
- One ask, no attachments

### Kickoff & Client Comms (post-signing)
Once the deal is closed, Sean's tone shifts slightly — warmer, still direct, more practical:
- "Sound, send me full spread sheet on Friday, don't go over 8 this week"
- "Hey mate. So I have cleaned up/fixed the [client.com] site..."
- Confirms next steps in writing within 24h of every call

### Internal Status Updates (Antonio)
- Short, direct, status-tagged
- "@Antonio 2 solid leads, we can discuss them on the call today. 1 more opportunity for tomorrow."
- "Been working hard to replace the lost deals."
- Casual is fine — "Fuck, just seeing this now" is on-brand for internal

## Sales Call Style (extracted from Otter.ai-summarised calls)

Sean's verbal pattern across discovery and pitch calls follows a consistent shape. Use this when drafting call recaps, role-play scripts, follow-up emails referencing what was said, or sales training material.

### Call open (rapport before pitch)
1. **Brief credibility story** — "I worked at Facebook's head office in Dublin, managing large ad budgets, and transitioned to freelancing." Always names the specific employer.
2. **Current focus** — "I now work with coaching companies in the US, high-ticket e-commerce coaching, focused on mindset coaching and full-funnel involvement."
3. **The partnership** — "I work with a colleague based in Malta whom I met at Facebook. We complement each other on marketing and accountability." (Always introduces Antonio for higher-stakes deals.)
4. **Question to the prospect** — turns the floor over before pitching anything.

### Discovery (always asks, never assumes)
Sean's discovery questions cluster around four areas. He asks them in roughly this order:

| Cluster | Sample questions Sean asks |
|---|---|
| **Current setup** | "What's your current ad spend?" / "Who manages your account today?" / "What CRM are you on?" |
| **Numbers** | "What's your CAC?" / "What's your average customer LTV?" / "What's your close rate from demo?" |
| **Gaps** | "Are you running retargeting?" / "Any lifecycle marketing post-purchase?" / "Is the pixel firing correctly?" |
| **Constraints** | "What's the timeline?" / "Who owns the decision?" / "What's already locked in?" |

### Sean's recurring observations during calls
These are framings he uses to position the diagnosis:
- "Agencies have limitations including the number of people working on a client's account."
- "Senior-level involvement matters more than agency support."
- "Strategy and messaging trump just media buying."
- "Long-term relationships outperform one-off engagements."
- "The ad is not the problem until the page is proven."
- "Two small changes (a talking-head video ad and slightly expanding ad-set targeting) produced ~5x improvements in book sales quickly." (Real recent client reference.)

### Recommendation pattern (every call)
Sean almost always lands on one of these recommendations regardless of the prospect:
1. **Deep-dive audit** as the first paid deliverable (~$1,500–3,000, 2 weeks)
2. **Then** a month-long campaign launch
3. **Then** transition to retainer or commission-based engagement
4. Pixel/tracking validation BEFORE any spend increase

He explicitly does NOT promise results before access. From his real Slack message: *"I don't think we should promise results (benchmarks to aim for) before we get under the hood and start working."*

### Pricing language (verbatim from real calls)
- "We typically charge about $60 per hour for that work."
- "Either a monthly agreement / retainer or time-and-materials billing where hours are tracked and billed."
- "$2–3k first-month estimate."
- "Retainer first, then move to commission-based around month 3 or 4."
- Always presents options. Never one number.

### Call structure he produces internally (Otter summary format)
After every sales call, Sean shares an Otter.ai link + structured summary in Slack. The structure:
- **Background** — who's on the call, location, basic context
- **Goals** — quantified targets ("$5K LTV", "30-38 customers/month")
- **Scope** — current funnel state, gaps
- **Success** — what good looks like, current metrics
- **Terms** — pricing options, engagement model
- **Action items** — list per person, with owner

This is internal-facing structure for briefing Antonio, NOT external communication. When drafting follow-up emails to prospects, strip this format and write prose.

### Follow-up email pattern (post-call)
Sean's follow-ups (drafted by Antonio, voice-aligned to Sean) use bold section headers + short prose answers. Each section ends with an action commitment. Example pattern from real Nineteenth Golf reply to client questions:

```
**Content:** [direct answer]. [supporting context]. [implication for the work].
**Volume:** [direct answer]. [framing of the structural choice].
**Landing page and pixel:** Yes, I'll handle the setup. [scope of setup]. This is critical to making the ads accountable from day one.
**What's next after the initial phase:** [conditional plan based on results].
**Existing accounts:** [acknowledgement of client's prep work].

Looking forward to speaking soon.
```

Always closes with a short forward-looking line — never "let me know if you have any questions."

## Anti-AI Patterns (avoid all of these)

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Instead |
|---|---|
| "Let's be real" / "Here's the truth" | Just state the point |
| "And honestly?" | Cut the qualifier |
| "Game-changing", "next-level", "10x" | A specific number |
| "Moreover," "Furthermore," "In addition" | "Also," "And," or new sentence |
| Forced rule of three | Use 2, 4, or 5 if it fits |
| Em dashes in marketing prose | Comma, colon, or full stop |
| "I'm thrilled to..." | Skip the emotion claim |
| "At the end of the day..." | Cut entirely |
| "Leverage" / "synergy" / "transform" | "use" / "fit" / "change" |

## Hard Rules (calibrated from real wins)

1. **Never open with "I'm excited" or "I'd love."** Sean's actual openers always start with experience, position, or the prospect's wording.
2. **Never claim a result without a number.** Always cite Chefs Office Academy or another verified case.
3. **Never write "let me know if you have any questions."** Use a conditional close: "If you are looking for someone who can [X], I would be a strong fit."
4. **Never copy a winning proposal verbatim** — always re-mirror the new prospect's situation in their wording.
5. **Always include a kill rule** in any analytical / diagnostic reply (the explicit condition for stopping the test).
6. **Mention Antonio for high-stakes / complex deals.** "I work with a colleague based in Malta whom I met at Facebook" — adds credibility on enterprise-style asks.
7. **Don't promise specific results before access.** Sean's stated rule: "I don't think we should promise results before we get under the hood and start working." (From real Slack message.)
8. **Match the prospect's structure.** If they ask for 4 bullets, give 4 bullets. If they ask for a paragraph, give a paragraph. Don't over-format.
9. **Sign off with "Seán"** (with the accent) on Upwork and email — not the full title block.
10. **External voice never uses profanity.** Internal Slack is fine — keep them strictly separate.
