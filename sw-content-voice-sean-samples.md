## 2. Alifeplus (DTC Food $8M+ — Performance Operator Role)

**Channel:** #lead-alifeplus
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**Stakes:** Senior performance role, $15-20k/mo Meta spend
**Result:** Won the screen — moved to call invitation

### Initial proposal

> I am not a task-based marketer and this role is a strong fit for how I naturally operate. I think in levers, not deliverables. When I come into an account or a business the first thing I do is identify which single metric, if moved, will have the greatest downstream impact on revenue, and then I design a controlled test to move it rather than running activity across multiple fronts simultaneously. I have worked directly with founders in DTC and ecommerce businesses where I was handed a number rather than a brief, and I am comfortable being judged on whether the number moves.

### Case study answer (CPL improvement)

> The single most impactful metric I have moved is cost per lead for an Australian education and coaching business, Chefs Office Academy. When I came into the account the lead generation campaigns were running without a dedicated low-friction capture layer, which meant the business was paying conversion campaign CPAs of $40 to $70 per buyer when the real opportunity was to seed the funnel cheaply with qualified leads and convert them downstream. I built a dedicated New Leads campaign optimised specifically for lead capture rather than purchase, using creative written in the language of the trade, and the result was 192 leads at $2.74 each on $527 in spend. That single structural change reduced the cost of filling the top of the funnel by more than 90 percent compared to relying on purchase campaigns alone to do all the work.

### Action/result detail

> The action was isolating the lead capture objective into its own campaign with its own creative brief, audience strategy, and budget, rather than asking one conversion campaign to serve both awareness and purchase intent simultaneously. I wrote ad creative specifically for people who were not yet ready to buy but had the problem the product solved, used a low-friction lead magnet as the entry point, and let the purchase and retargeting campaigns work against the warm pool that campaign built. The result was 192 leads at $2.74 CPL, 77 ebook purchases from the broader funnel, and 20 direct sales conversations generated from a geo-specific messaging campaign targeting restaurant owners in Perth at $19.64 per conversation. Total visible spend across the account was $4,782, which produced measurable output at every stage of the funnel rather than concentrating results at one conversion point.

### CAC/CVR/retention paragraph

> I work with CAC as the primary operating metric on every ecommerce and DTC account I manage, cross-referenced against contribution margin and payback period rather than blended ROAS which can mask poor new customer economics. On CVR I have optimised landing pages, offer structure, and funnel sequencing for DTC brands including running structured single-variable tests on headlines, CTA copy, and page layout to identify what is causing drop-off before increasing ad spend. On retention I have worked with Klaviyo flows including post-purchase sequences, winback campaigns, and replenishment triggers for consumable product brands, and I understand how retention rate and repeat purchase frequency change the CAC threshold a business can afford to operate at, which is where DTC profitability at $8M plus revenue is usually won or lost.

**Voice notes:**
- Mirrors prospect's exact wording ("task-based marketer", "operator")
- Specific case study with full numbers
- Reframes blended ROAS as misleading
- Owns the methodology (single-variable testing, kill rules)

## 3. Alifeplus Blind Diagnostic (4-bullet format requested)

**Channel:** #lead-alifeplus
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**Format:** Constrained — no decks, no frameworks, max 4 bullets, 48-hour turnaround
**Result:** "It scored well on our side and we'd like to move forward."

> Hi George,
>
> Happy to work through this blind.
>
> The first 3 metrics I'd verify and why:
>
> New customer CAC by channel and campaign, not blended. Blended CAC at a subscription business hides which acquisition source is profitable and which is subsidised by retention revenue from older cohorts.
>
> Landing page CVR by traffic source. If paid traffic is converting at a materially different rate than organic or direct, the problem is either audience mismatch or page experience, and that tells me where to act first.
>
> Add to cart or initiate checkout rate versus purchase rate. A high drop between those two events tells me the friction is at the offer, price, or trust layer, not the ad. A low add to cart rate tells me the ad is attracting the wrong intent.
>
> The first trigger I'd act on:
> If CVR from paid traffic is below 1.5 percent on a subscription offer, I act on the landing page before touching the ad account. Increasing ad spend into a weak conversion rate accelerates CAC bleed. The ad is not the problem until the page is proven.
>
> The single first test or change I'd run:
> Replace or rewrite the above-the-fold section of the landing page with a single variable test: one version leading with the outcome the customer gets, one version leading with the problem it solves. No other variables change. Traffic split 50/50 from the existing best performing ad set.
>
> Expected result within 72 hours and the kill rule:
> With sufficient traffic I expect to see a statistically meaningful signal on CVR within 72 hours. If neither variant hits a CVR above the current baseline after 200 sessions each, I kill both, revert to control, and move the test one step upstream to the ad creative hook, which means the drop off is happening before the page rather than on it.
>
> Seán

**Voice notes:**
- Opens with: "Happy to work through this blind." — short, direct
- Each metric paired with WHY ("Blended CAC at a subscription business hides...")
- Threshold-based triggers ("If CVR from paid traffic is below 1.5%...")
- Explicit kill rule (this is rare among other applicants — strong differentiator)
- Sign off: "Seán" with accent
