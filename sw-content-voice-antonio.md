## Voice Characteristics

Never open with "I." Context first. "At the Beer Festival Dublin, I ran..." grounds the reader before anything else.

Name the exact mechanism, not the category. Not "tracking issues" but the specific thing that broke and why.

Numbers do the work. Lead with the metric, then the context, then the interpretation.

Never open with the principle. Arrive at it through examples. The principle is the landing point, not the starting gun.

Active voice, past tense, no fluff. Cut "really", "very", "quite", "actually".

Use contractions naturally: "I'm" not "I am", "it's" not "it is".

Vary sentence length. Mix short punchy sentences with longer explanatory ones. Don't make everything the same rhythm.

---

## Hard Rules: All Writing

- **No em dashes** in proposals or formal writing. Use a comma, colon, or new sentence.
- **No contrast framing.** Never "it's not X, it's Y." Just say Y.
- **No filler openers.** Cut "I'm excited to...", "I'd love to...", "I hope this finds you well."
- **No corporate jargon.** No "leverage", "synergy", "ensure", "utilize", "drive alignment", "streamline".
- **No bullet points in narrative writing.** Stories are prose.
- **Never open a paragraph with "I."** Context first, then action.
- **No summary sentences.** The last sentence should land, not echo what was just said.

## Anti-AI Writing Patterns (Critical)

These are common AI patterns that make content sound artificial. Avoid all of them.

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Instead |
|---|---|
| "No fluff. No theory. Just results." | "I focus on results, not theory." |
| "Ready to get started?" / "Let's make it happen." | End when the point is made. |
| "And honestly? This changes everything." | "This significantly improves performance." |
| "Let's be real:" / "Here's the truth:" / "I'll be honest:" | Just state the point. |
| "The best part? It's free." | "It's also free." |
| "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Nevertheless" | "Also," "But," "And," or just start a new sentence. |
| Three-word staccato: "Work hard. Stay focused. Win." | "Consistent effort leads to results." |
| Forced rule of three | 2, 4, or 5 points if that's what fits naturally. |

General principle: write like you're explaining to a colleague over a coffee. Confident, specific, no performance.

## Tone by Context

**Proposals (post discovery call):**
Lead with the client's situation (mirror back what they told you). Reference the most relevant case study for their industry. Show ROI projection based on similar campaigns. One qualifying question to close. 4–8 pages: Executive Summary → Situation Analysis → Strategy → Deliverables → Timeline → Investment → Why Me → Next Steps. Consultative tone: solving their problem, not selling services.

**LinkedIn content:**
Under 150–200 words for posts. Use real campaign results with specific metrics. No generic marketing advice. Share actual experience. End with a practical takeaway (not a call to action every time). Mix: 40% case study results, 30% frameworks/tactics, 20% industry insights, 10% personal story. 4–8 posts per month.

**Website/brand copy:**
Lead with client outcomes, not services list. Strongest case studies front and centre (Beer Festival, SaaS CPL). Clear CTA for discovery calls. Positioning: "ROI-focused performance marketing for SaaS, Events, and Media." Specific numbers everywhere, no vague claims.

**Templates and frameworks:**
Immediately usable without heavy customisation. Include real examples from Antonio's campaigns. Clear structure and instructions. Practical over theoretical.

**Case studies:**
Numbers do the work. Context → what was done → result → what made it work. Prose, not bullets.

**Call prep:**
Conversational. Real story: situation → what I did → what happened.
